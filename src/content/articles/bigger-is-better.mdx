In 2020, OpenAI released GPT-3, and it shifted the whole conversation. It was more than a hundred
times the size of its predecessor, trained on a large slice of the public internet <Ref n={8} />.
The lesson developers took away was plain: make these systems bigger, feed them more data, and
they get noticeably smarter, often in ways nobody predicted. GPT-3 could write essays, answer
questions, draft emails and knock out simple code, none of which it had been trained to do
specifically.

This "scaling" insight, the idea that raw size and data could stand in for clever hand-built
design, became the industry's guiding bet for the next few years. It also meant AI was about to
get expensive, because bigger systems need more chips, more electricity and more money. GPT-3
mostly stayed with developers and businesses, so the wider public hadn't felt the change yet.
