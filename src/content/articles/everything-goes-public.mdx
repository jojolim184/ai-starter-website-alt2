For five years this had mostly played out away from the public. In 2022 it broke into the open.

The first wave was visual. Stable Diffusion and Midjourney, both of which reached the public in
2022, let anyone type a sentence and get back a striking image seconds later <Ref n={11} />. Social
media filled up with AI art almost overnight, and artists and photographers started warning that
their work was being copied and their livelihoods put at risk.

Then, on 30 November 2022, OpenAI released ChatGPT <Ref n={12} />. Underneath, it was a friendly
chat window sitting on top of the GPT technology the company had spent years refining. The plain
conversational wrapper is what changed everything. Ordinary people could now ask an AI to explain
an idea, write a poem, plan a trip or fix their code, and get back an answer that felt unnervingly
human. ChatGPT passed an estimated hundred million users in about two months, one of the fastest
take-ups of any product on record <Ref n={12} />. This was the moment "AI" entered the everyday
vocabulary of hundreds of millions of people. The race was public now, and it was on.
