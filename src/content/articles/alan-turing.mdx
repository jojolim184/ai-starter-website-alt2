Long before anyone used the phrase artificial intelligence, Alan Turing was already turning over
the idea of a thinking machine. <Cite n={1}>Turing was the mathematician who cracked Enigma</Cite>,
the German cipher machine used in the Second World War <Ref n={1} />. After the war he helped
design some of the earliest computers, laying groundwork that still sits under the technology we
use today <Ref n={1} />. In 1950 he took on the harder question of whether a machine could think,
and put forward <Cite n={2}>the Turing Test</Cite> as a way of deciding whether one was really
"intelligent" <Ref n={2} />.

The phrase itself arrived soon after. It was coined in <Cite n={3}>the 1955 proposal for the
Dartmouth Workshop</Cite>, which ran the following year and is usually treated as the birth of the
field <Ref n={3} />. The two decades from 1956 to 1976 brought real progress, most notably
<Cite n={4}>the perceptron</Cite>, an early ancestor of the neural networks that everything now
rests on <Ref n={4} />.
