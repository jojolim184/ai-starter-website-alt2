Then it stalled. Funding dried up and the criticism piled on, and
<Cite n={5}>the stretch from 1974 to 1980 became known as the "AI Winter"</Cite> <Ref n={5} />. AI
only really came back into public view with Deep Blue, the IBM chess machine that famously beat
grandmaster Garry Kasparov <Ref n={5} />.

From there the pace picked up. Between 2005 and 2017 the field sped along, helped by two things
arriving at once: an ocean of data thrown off by everything we did on computers and the internet,
and hardware that kept getting cheaper and more capable. That window produced AlexNet, the rise of
deep neural networks, and AlphaGo <Ref n={6} />.

Arguably the biggest shift of the lot is the transformer, introduced in
<Cite n={7}>the now-famous paper "Attention Is All You Need"</Cite> <Ref n={7} />. It rewrote how
these networks were built, and it became the backbone of the large language models (LLMs) we all
use today <Ref n={7} />.
