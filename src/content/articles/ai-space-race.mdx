The pace, already frantic, tipped into something almost comical late in 2025. Across about three
weeks in November and December, four of the biggest labs each put out their most powerful system
yet: xAI's Grok 4.1, then Google's Gemini 3 a day later, then Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.5, then
OpenAI's GPT-5.2 <Ref n={27} />. Google pushed Gemini 3 to billions of search users on day one
<Ref n={28} />. Each launch was upstaged by the next before anyone had time to take it in.
Commentators, only half-joking, called the tempo "singularity speed", the point where progress
compounds faster than anyone can keep track of <Ref n={27} />.

That rhythm carried straight into 2026. Anthropic in particular shipped update after update, Opus
4.6 and Sonnet 4.6 in February, Opus 4.7 in April and Opus 4.8 in May, while OpenAI, Google and
xAI matched it release for release <Ref n={29} />. Anthropic also brought in a new top tier of
especially capable models under the "Mythos" name, at first only for a small set of trusted
organisations doing safety-sensitive work <Ref n={29} />. By the middle of 2026 the upshot is a
field where the "best model" changes month to month and depends heavily on what you're trying to
do.

China's momentum hasn't let up either. By 2026 its AI scene is no longer a one-company story.
DeepSeek, Alibaba's Qwen, Moonshot's Kimi, ByteDance's Doubao, Zhipu's GLM and Baidu's ERNIE all
compete on different strengths, most of them shipping open-weight models that match American
systems on price <Ref n={25} />. In July 2026 Moonshot released Kimi K3 and billed it as the
largest open-source model in the world, a sign of how firmly China now leads on open, freely
available AI, even while some markets stay wary about trusting models built in China <Ref n={30} />.

Underneath the model races, the money kept swelling. The five largest American cloud and AI
infrastructure companies, Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta and Oracle, together lined up somewhere
between $660 and $690 billion in spending for 2026 alone, close to double the year before
<Ref n={31} />. Stargate's first data centres came online in Texas and broke ground in Michigan
and beyond <Ref n={[22, 32]} />. The bubble argument sharpened along with it. Sceptics point to
circular financing and enormous costs that revenue hasn't justified yet, while believers say the
productivity gains are real and still only starting <Ref n={23} />. As of the middle of 2026 the
bubble hasn't burst, though the question of whether it will hangs over the whole industry.
