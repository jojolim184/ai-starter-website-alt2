With the big players in place, 2024 was about making the systems faster, stronger and more natural
to use, and about one new capability that changed the game.

In the northern spring, OpenAI released GPT-4o, which could see, hear and speak close to real
time, holding a spoken conversation with barely a pause <Ref n={8} />. Around the same time
Anthropic shipped its Claude 3 family, then an upgraded Claude 3.5 that developers took to for its
coding <Ref n={[14, 16]} />. Google's Gemini models learned to handle enormous inputs in one go,
whole books or hours of video at a time. Late in the year Anthropic gave Claude an early knack for
operating a computer directly, moving a cursor and clicking, an early look at where things were
headed <Ref n={16} />.

The bigger shift was quieter. In September, OpenAI introduced a system nicknamed o1, built on a
simple idea: rather than blurting out the first answer it landed on, the model would take extra
time to work through a problem step by step, the way a person scribbles on scratch paper before
answering something hard <Ref n={8} />. These became known as reasoning models, and they were much
better at maths, science and logic puzzles. Nearly every major lab picked up the approach soon
after. Bigger was no longer the only path to smarter. Thinking for longer was now on the menu as
well.

2024 was also when the Chinese players moved from the margins toward the centre. Alibaba's Qwen
models became some of the most downloaded open-source systems in the world, so common that when a
developer anywhere fine-tuned "an open model", it was very often a Qwen underneath <Ref n={17} />.
And a little-known startup founded in 2023, DeepSeek, put out a strong model late in the year that
caught the experts' eye <Ref n={18} />. Few outside the field saw what was coming.
