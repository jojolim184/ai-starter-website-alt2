# Writing Guide

Make prose read as the work of a considered human writer rather than as machine-generated
text. This skill governs style and diction only. It does not restructure arguments, reorder
sections, add claims, or change what the writing says.

## When to apply

Any request to rewrite, tighten, or de-slop prose, and any prose being drafted for
ClearAI, including website copy, newsletter material, and client-facing documents.
Recognise the pattern rather than the exact wording. If the output is prose a reader will
judge, apply this skill.

## The rules

### Do not use em dashes

The em dash character, which is the long dash sometimes used in place of commas, brackets,
or colons, should not appear anywhere in the output. This rule has no exceptions. Where the
source uses an em dash, restructure the sentence so the same idea is carried by a comma, a
semicolon, a colon, a pair of brackets, or by splitting the material into properly formed
complete sentences. The hyphen inside compound words such as "client-facing" is unaffected.

Em dashes have become one of the most reliable signals that a piece of prose was generated
by an AI system, so even where they would be grammatically defensible they now carry a
reputational cost.

### Favour complete sentences over short fragments

Avoid stacking several short sentences in a row when the same content can be expressed as a
single well-formed sentence. Use connectors such as "because", "which", "while", "where",
"and", "but", and "so that" to bind related ideas into one flowing sentence. The aim is
prose that reads with rhythm and authority rather than prose that feels clipped.

This does not mean every sentence should be long. A short sentence is appropriate when it
carries genuine weight, such as a closing assertion or a single sharp finding. The rule is
against habitual fragmentation, not against deliberate brevity.

### Use Australian English

Spelling and idiom follow Australian English conventions, in line with house style:
"organise" rather than "organize", "behaviour" rather than "behavior", "centre" rather
than "center", "programme" for a structured initiative, and similar forms throughout.

### Vary sentence rhythm

Uniform sentence length is itself a tell. Language models tend to produce paragraphs where
every sentence runs to a similar length and follows a similar clause pattern. Vary the
length and construction deliberately so the paragraph has a shape a human writer would have
given it.

## AI tells to remove

Each of these patterns is recognised on sight by readers in marketing, consulting, and
editorial settings, and each costs credibility even when the underlying analysis is sound.

**Forced contrast framings.** "It is not just X, it is Y." "This is not about X, it is
about Y." "X is only half the story." The construction manufactures tension that is not in
the material. State the point directly instead.

**Rule-of-three stacking.** Three adjectives, three clauses, or three-item lists used as a
default rhythm rather than because there are exactly three things. Use the number of items
the content actually has.

**Cliché openers.** "In today's fast-paced world", "In an era of", "Let's dive in", "Here's
the thing", "At its core", "The reality is". Open with the substance.

**Buzzword vocabulary.** "Leverage", "unlock", "harness", "seamless", "robust",
"game-changing", "revolutionary", "delve", "landscape", "realm", "tapestry", "testament to",
"navigate the complexities of", "in the ever-evolving world of". Replace with plain words
that name the specific thing.

**Hollow intensifiers and hedges used together.** "Truly transformative", "incredibly
powerful", "arguably one of the most significant". Either the claim is supported or it is
cut.

**Summary scaffolding the reader did not ask for.** Restating what a section just said
before moving on, or closing every section with a one-line takeaway. Say it once.

**Decorative emojis** in professional prose, and heading emoji used as visual filler.

**Symmetrical paragraph architecture.** Every paragraph opening with a topic sentence of
similar shape and closing with a similar reflective line. Real writing is less tidy.

**Over-signposting.** "Firstly", "Secondly", "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally"
stacked as connective tissue where the logic is already clear from the content.

## Process

1. Read the draft in full before editing anything.
2. Remove every em dash and repunctuate or restructure the affected sentence.
3. Join clipped fragments into complete sentences where doing so improves the flow.
4. Work through the AI-tells list above and rewrite each hit, replacing the pattern with a
   direct statement of the underlying point rather than deleting the sentence outright.
5. Correct spelling and idiom to Australian English.
6. Read each paragraph and ask whether it could have been produced by a generic language
   model without any human shaping. If it could, rewrite it.
7. If any rewrite changed meaning rather than only style, flag it in a short note at the end
   of the response, outside the rewritten text.

The standard is whether a reader familiar with AI-generated writing would read the result as
the work of a human author.
